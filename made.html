Made with AI: an experiment

The Spectre Knot is an experiment in using AI for mathematical exploration. Justin Roughley came up with the original idea of looking for a mono‑stranded aperiodic knot, and then used AI to search for it. From then on he and an AI system (Claude, by Anthropic) worked together at every stage: the AI did the detailed work, and he directed it, guided it and corrected it. Together they searched for the design, proved the two theorems, wrote a formal version of the proof that the Lean proof assistant checks, and made the paper, this website and the handout.

The status of the proof

The proofs and the Lean were written by the AI, working with Justin Roughley. Lean checks the combinatorial heart of the proof; the step linking it to actual tilings of the plane (how real supertiles fit together) is argued in words. Justin Roughley has read parts of the write-up and spot-checked pictures and counts; most of the proof has not been reviewed by a human, and no professional mathematician has reviewed it.

AI can turn out a great deal of plausible-looking mathematics very quickly. That is why the proof was also written for Lean, whose checker verifies every step mechanically, and why this page sets out plainly who did what.

A similar approach used to formalise Fermat’s Last Theorem

In September 2026 Anthropic described how Claude wrote a complete Lean proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem, building on Lean’s mathematical library: Formalizing Fermat’s Last Theorem. Kevin Buzzard (Imperial College London) reviewed it and said that it proves the theorem with no assumptions beyond the axioms of mathematics. The Spectre Knot’s Lean is far smaller but works the same way: the AI writes the proof, and Lean’s checker verifies every step. While the Spectre Knot’s Lean does reach all the way down to the axioms for most of the steps, it also relies on the way real supertiles fit together in the plane, as described in this paper: A chiral aperiodic monotile.

What has been checked

By Lean. The combinatorial part of the proof is written out in Lean, a language for proofs whose checker verifies every step mechanically, and Lean accepts it: that every supertile, of every kind and size, looks the same from outside; that no strand closes into a loop; and that in the nest tiling all the pieces join into one strand. It takes as given how real supertiles fit together in the plane, from the spectre paper; that step is argued in words.

By computer. Every computation the proof relies on has been re-run in full, with logs kept. The geometric computations were done twice, by two separate programs, one in floating point and one in exact arithmetic.

By the AI. The AI reviewed the written argument several times, and the errors it found were fixed.

What has not

Most of the proof has not been reviewed by a human, and no professional mathematician has reviewed it. The Prove page marks each step as cited from the spectre paper, computed, or argued in words, with a Lean tag where Lean also checks it, and says exactly which tilings each theorem covers.

A note on words

Where this site says ‘we’, it means Justin Roughley and the AI working together. Every program, including the second or separate ones used as checks, and the Lean, was written by the AI.
