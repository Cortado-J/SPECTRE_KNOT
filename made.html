Made with AI: an experiment

The Spectre Knot is an experiment in using AI for mathematical exploration. Justin Roughley came up with the original idea of looking for a mono‑stranded aperiodic knot, and then used AI to search for it. From then on an AI system (Claude, by Anthropic) did the detailed work at every stage, always at his instruction: it generated the programs, the proofs of the two theorems, a formal version of the proof that the Lean proof assistant checks, the paper, this website and the handout. He set the direction, made the choices, questioned the work and had it corrected. Justin Roughley is the author, and is responsible for the work, including its correctness; the AI is not an author.

The status of the proof

The proofs and the Lean were generated by the AI at Justin Roughley’s instruction. Lean checks the combinatorial heart of the proof; the step linking it to actual tilings of the plane (how real supertiles fit together) is argued in words. Justin Roughley has read parts of the paper and spot-checked pictures and counts; most of the proof has not been reviewed by a human, and no professional mathematician has reviewed it. No human has checked that the Lean’s statements say what the theorems say. The paper has not been peer reviewed.

AI can turn out a great deal of plausible-looking mathematics very quickly. That is why the proof was also written for Lean, whose checker verifies every step mechanically, and why this page sets out plainly who did what.

What has been checked

What has been checked, by Lean, by computer, by the AI and by Justin Roughley, and what has not, is set out on the proof site: What has been checked. On this site, the Prove page marks each step as cited from the spectre paper, computed, or argued in words, with a Lean tag where Lean also checks it, and says exactly which tilings each theorem covers. Every program, including the second or separate ones used as checks, and the Lean, was generated by the same AI system. Every tool used is listed in the paper’s Tool and computational resource disclosure.
