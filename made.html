Made with AI: an experiment

This project is an experiment in using AI for mathematical exploration. I came up with the idea of looking for a mono‑stranded aperiodic knot. Then I used an AI system (Claude, by Anthropic) for almost everything else: the search for a design, the computations, the proof, the paper, this website and the handout.

The proof has not been checked by a person

I have not checked the whole proof, and parts of it are beyond my expertise. No mathematician has reviewed it. I’m sharing it as it is, with that said plainly, so you can decide for yourself how much weight to give it. The tile itself is there to enjoy: print it, make a few, and see the ribbons join up.

AI can turn out a great deal of plausible-looking mathematics very quickly. I would rather be open about how this was made, so this page sets out plainly who did what.

What has been checked

Every computation the proof relies on has been re-run in full, with logs kept. The geometric computations were done twice, by two separate programs, one in floating point and one in exact arithmetic, and so were the induction step and the computation showing that every block of the nest joins into one strand. All of these programs were written by the AI, so they are separate implementations, not independent people. The AI also reviewed the written argument several times, acting as a referee, and the errors it found were fixed.

What has not

No one with the right expertise has checked the argument, so it is a claim, not an established result. The Prove page marks each step as cited from the spectre paper, computed, or argued in words, and says exactly which tilings the claim covers.

A note on words

Where this site says ‘we’, it means me working with the AI. Where it says a check was done by a second or separate program, that program was also written by the AI.
