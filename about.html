How this was made

Justin Roughley came up with the original idea of looking for a mono‑stranded aperiodic knot, and then used AI (Claude, by Anthropic) to search for it. The search produced 34 candidate designs; he chose design 0.1:01 and drew its ribbons by hand in a tile editor.

AI was also used, at his direction, for the proof and the programs behind it, and for building this website, its content and its design. The proof has not been checked by a mathematician. Made with AI sets out who did what, and what has and has not been checked.

What is checked, and how

The two claims, the Endless Strand Theorem (no strand ever closes into a loop, in any spectre tiling whose supertiles fill the plane) and the Ariadne Theorem (it is one strand, in the nest tiling drawn on this site), rest on computer checks plus a written argument, developed by AI, that uses theorems from the spectre paper. The Prove page sets out each step, marks what is computed, what is cited and what is argued, and states what is not covered.
